# The AI Build-Out Is Sucking Up Resources and Crowding Out the Rest of the Economy

---

The artificial-intelligence build-out has become the major force behind U.S. economic growth. But in doing so it is also pushing other areas of the economy aside.

Anthropic's tumultuous performance dragged down the whole market on Friday, just a day after debuting the largest public offering ever. The selloff, combined with problems at chip maker Nvidia, underscored risks to an economy so dependent on AI for growth.

The build-out is far-reaching. There is spending on data-center construction, on semiconductors and computing gear, on generators, backup batteries and cooling systems. Hotels near data centers are seeing revenue growth rise.

"The tentacles of AI are remarkable," said UBS economist Jonathan Pingle. He estimates that the AI boom has accounted for more than half of the nation's economic growth over the past year.

Columbia University economist Stijn Van Nieuwerburgh estimates that nearly $1.3 trillion will go into the AI build-out alone in the U.S. this year. That is more than 1.6 times the gross domestic product of his native Belgium.

U.S. AI-infrastructure companies have issued $212 billion in stock this year through October, according to S&P Global Market Intelligence.

Many of the things AI is vacuuming up—chips and electricity, labor and money—are resources that other businesses also need. The result is a version of what economists call "crowding out."

### Chips
The impact is most evident in surging prices for the memory and storage chips—especially those known as DRAM and NAND flash memory—that go into data centers. Some NAND products have more than quadrupled in price over the past year, according to FactSet.

These same components also go into a slew of popular consumer-electronics products, leading to higher prices for items such as iPhones and Nintendo Switches. As chip costs have gone up, "we have had to pass those price increases through to customers," said Nirav Patel, chief executive of personal-computer maker Framework Computer.

The San-Francisco-based company has paused production on chip-powered memory expansion cards it lists at $50 and $125, letting them sell out. The company said that it would have had to raise prices beyond what most customers would willingly pay.

### Money
So far this year, the so-called hyperscalers—Alphabet, Amazon.com, Meta Platforms, Microsoft and Oracle—have issued $277 billion in debt, according to Fitch Ratings, which compares with $108 billion for all of last year. On top of that, data centers have also issued billions of dollars of debt.

AI debt has the potential to affect other types of corporate debt, however. AI debt issuance has accounted for about a fifth of debt issuance this year, up from about a 10th in 2025, according to Dealogic and Goldman.

In a recent report, Fitch analysts cautioned that the heavy concentration of AI-related debt could make the broader bond market more volatile. If investors continue to sour on AI debt, Friday's turmoil could spread. Companies in other sectors could end up paying more to borrow, especially those that have to refinance large amounts of debt soon.
